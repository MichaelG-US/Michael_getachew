My Story

From the field to the cloud

"Data without context is noise. My career has been about turning field reality into strategic signal."
15+ Years in Humanitarian Data
4 Countries Deployed
6 UN & INGO Positions
5 Major Donors Served

I started my career as a researcher trying to understand how communities survive and adapt in Ethiopia. That curiosity about people and systems led me into monitoring and evaluation — the discipline that asks not just "did we do what we said we'd do?" but "did it actually matter?"

Over the following decade, I built M&E systems across some of the world's most complex humanitarian contexts: South Sudan during active conflict, Ethiopia during the GTP II agricultural transformation, and Ukraine at the height of the 2022–2025 crisis. Each assignment demanded that I translate chaotic ground-level data into clear narratives for organizations like UNFPA, UNHCR, the Gates Foundation, USAID, EU, and DFID.

The most meaningful work I've done has been at this intersection — between the human stories in the field and the decision-makers who control the resources. A well-designed indicator framework, a rigorously validated dataset, or a dashboard that actually gets read by a minister: these are the things that shift how funding flows and how lives are protected.

Now I'm deliberately expanding my technical toolkit. The AWS re/Start bootcamp at Per Scholas, a Data Analytics certificate from eCornell, and ongoing Python and SQL study aren't career pivots — they're depth additions. I want to work in organizations where my M&E instincts and my growing cloud and analytics skills can amplify each other, particularly in global health, public sector technology, and philanthropic impact measurement.

I'm based in Bellevue, WA — fully work-authorized, no sponsorship needed — and actively looking for roles where data and impact are both taken seriously.

How I Work

Principles that guide me

01
Evidence Over Anecdote
Fifteen years of humanitarian work taught me that good intentions aren't enough. Every decision should be traceable to data — collected rigorously, analyzed honestly, and communicated without spin.
02
Context is Everything
Numbers without field context mislead. I've sat in refugee camps, government offices, and donor briefings — I know how differently the same data reads depending on where you're standing.
03
Always Be Learning
The humanitarian sector taught me humility. The tech sector is teaching me velocity. Holding both — deep expertise in one domain and beginner's mind in another — is where I do my best thinking.
